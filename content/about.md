## Hello, I'm Joyce, and yes, I forget things too

I would love to tell you that I walked into cybersecurity with a plan, a perfect study schedule, and a laptop that cooperates. What actually happened is that I studied microbiology, wandered into security, and then spent months learning things, forgetting them, and relearning them with a lot more respect. This site is where I keep the receipts.

## The honest version of learning

Nobody warns you that learning security is mostly a cycle of confidence, confusion, and a quiet "wait, how did I do that last week?" I have paused a platform because it felt like it was rushing me through knowledge I hadn't absorbed, picked up a textbook to go slower, and discovered that slow was the fastest thing I did all year. I have watched Kali Linux lag on my laptop with the energy of a very tired snail, and I have learned that a lab that refuses to load is also a lesson, mostly in patience and in moving things to the cloud.

## Unlearning is half the work

I started out pointed at penetration testing, and I have since turned towards DevSecOps. That was not a failure, it was information. Breaking things taught me how attackers think, and now I'm learning to build and automate things so they are harder to break. I finished one TryHackMe path, I'm about halfway through the next, and the biggest thing I've had to unlearn is the idea that changing direction means starting from zero.

## What you'll find here

The [**Projects**](/projects/) page is where I build things in public, including Northgate Freight, a fictional company that I attack and then try to defend. The [**Blog**](/posts/) is where my CTF write-ups and study notes live, along with the mistakes, the dead ends, and the things I forgot and had to learn again. If a post ends with "and then it worked," please assume it took at least three attempts.
